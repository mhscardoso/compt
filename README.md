> I am nothing.
> I will never be anything.
> I cannot want to be anything.
> Apart from that, I hold within me all the dreams of the world.
> Windows of my room,
> Of my room, one of millions in the world whose identity is unknown,
> (And if they knew who it was, what would they know?),
> You open onto the mystery of a street constantly crossed by people,
> Onto a street inaccessible to all thoughts,
> Real, impossibly real, certain, unknowingly certain,
> With the mystery of things beneath the stones and beings,
> With death putting dampness on the walls and white hairs on men,
> With Destiny driving the cart of everything along the road of nothing.
> Today I am defeated, as if I knew the truth.
> I am lucid today, as if I were about to die,
> And had no more kinship with things
> Except for a farewell, this house and this side of the street becoming
> The row of carriages of a train, and a whistled departure
> From inside my head,
> And a jolt of my nerves and a creaking of bones on the way.
> I am perplexed today like someone who thought and found and forgot.
> I am divided today between the loyalty I owe
> To the Tobacco Shop across the street, as something real on the outside,
> And the feeling that everything is a dream, as something real on the inside.
> I failed in everything.
> Since I made no purpose, perhaps everything was nothing.
> The learning they gave me,
> I descended from it through the back window of the house,
> I went to the countryside with great purposes.
> But there I found only grass and trees,
> And when there were people, they were all the same.
> I leave the window, I sit in a chair. What should I think about?
> What do I know of what I will be, I who do not know what I am?
> To be what I think? But I think I am so many things!
> And there are so many who think they are the same thing that there cannot be so many!
> Genius? At this moment
> A hundred thousand brains conceive themselves in dreams as geniuses like me,
> And history will not record, who knows?, even one,
> Nor will there be anything but manure from so many future conquests.
> No, I don't believe in myself.
> In every madhouse there are crazy lunatics with so many certainties!
> I, who have no certainty, am I more certain or less certain?
> No, not even in myself...
> In how many attics and non-attics of the world
> Are there not self-proclaimed geniuses dreaming at this very moment?
> How many lofty, noble, and lucid aspirations—
> Yes, truly lofty, noble, and lucid—
> And who knows if they are achievable,
> Will they never see the light of the real sun nor find human ears?
> The world is for those born to conquer it,
> And not for those who dream they can conquer it, even if they are right.
> I have dreamed more than Napoleon did.
> I have held more hypothetical humanities to my breast than Christ,
> I have secretly created philosophies that no Kant ever wrote.
> But I am, and perhaps will always be, the one in the attic,
> Even if I don't live there;
> I will always be the one who wasn't born for this;
> I will always be only the one who had qualities;
> I will always be the one who waited for someone to open the door next to a wall without a door,
> And sang the song of Infinity in a chicken coop,
> And heard the voice of God in a covered well. Believe in me? No, not in anything.
> Let Nature pour down upon my burning head
> Its sun, its rain, the wind that finds my hair,
> And let the rest come if it comes, or has to come, or not come at all.
> Heart slaves of the stars,
> We conquered the whole world before we got out of bed;
> But we woke up and it is opaque,
> We got up and it is alien,
> We left home and it is the whole earth,
> Plus the solar system and the Milky Way and the Indefinite.
> (Eat chocolates, little one;
> Eat chocolates!
> Look, there's no more metaphysics in the world but chocolates.
> Look, all religions teach nothing more than confectionery.
> Eat, you dirty little thing, eat!
> If only I could eat chocolates with the same truth with which you eat!
> But I think, and as I remove the silver paper, which is tin foil,
> I throw everything to the ground, as I have thrown my life.)
> But at least there remains of the bitterness of what I will never be
> The quick calligraphy of these verses,
> A broken portico to the Impossible.
> But at least I consecrate to myself a tearless contempt,
> Noble at least in the broad gesture with which I throw
> The dirty clothes that I am, without a roll, into the course of things,
> And I stay home shirtless.
> (You, who console, who do not exist and therefore console,
> Or a Greek goddess, conceived as a living statue,
> Or a Roman patrician, impossibly noble and nefarious,
> Or a princess of troubadours, most gentle and colorful,
> Or a marchioness of the eighteenth century, low-cut and distant,
> Or a celebrated courtesan from the time of our parents,
> Or some modern thing—I can't quite conceive what—,
> All of this, whatever you may be, may you inspire, inspire!
> My heart is an emptied bucket.
> Like those who invoke spirits, I invoke spirits,
> Myself, and I find nothing.
> I go to the window and see the street with absolute clarity.
> I see the shops, I see the sidewalks, I see the passing cars,
> I see the living beings dressed, crossing paths,
> I see the dogs that also exist,
> And all this weighs on me like a condemnation.) into exile,
> And all this is foreign, like everything else.)
> I lived, I studied, I loved, and I even created,
> And today there is no beggar I don't envy simply for not being me.
> I look at each one's rags and wounds and lies,
> And I think: perhaps you never lived nor studied nor loved nor believed
> (Because it's possible to make the reality of all that without doing any of it);
> Perhaps you only existed, like a lizard whose tail is cut off
> And which is a tail beyond the lizard wriggling.
> I made of myself what I didn't know how to be,
> And what I could have made of myself I didn't.
> The domino I wore was wrong.
> They knew me immediately for who I wasn't and I didn't deny it, and I lost myself.
> When I wanted to take off the mask,
> It was stuck to my face.
> When I took it off and saw myself in the mirror,
> I had already aged.
> I was drunk, I no longer knew how to wear the domino I hadn't taken off.
> I threw away the mask and slept in the dressing room
> Like a dog tolerated by management
> For being harmless
> And I will write this story to prove that I am sublime.
> Musical essence of my useless verses,
> I wish I could find you as something I made,
> And not always stand in front of the tobacconist's shop across the street,
> Trampling underfoot the awareness of existing,
> Like a rug a drunk stumbles over
> Or a doormat the gypsies stole that was worthless.
> But the owner of the tobacconist's shop arrived at the door and stood there.
> He looked at him with the discomfort of a head turned awkwardly
> And with the discomfort of a soul misunderstanding.
> He will die and I will die.
> He will leave the sign, and I will leave verses.
> At a certain point the sign will die too, and the verses too.
> After a certain point, the street where the sign stood will die,
> And the language in which the verses were written.
> Then the spinning planet where all this happened will die.
> On other satellites of other systems, something like people
> Will continue doing things like verses and living under things like signs,
> Always one thing facing another,
> Always one thing as useless as the other,
> Always the impossible as stupid as the real,
> Always the mystery of the depths as certain as the sleep of mystery on the surface,
> Always this or always something else or neither one thing nor the other.
> But a man entered the Tobacco Shop (to buy tobacco?),
> And plausible reality suddenly falls upon me.
> I half-rise energetically, convinced, human,
> And I intend to write these verses in which I say the opposite.
> I light a cigarette while thinking about writing them
> And I savor in the cigarette the liberation of all thoughts.
> I follow the smoke as if it were my own path,
> And I enjoy, in a sensitive and competent moment,
> The liberation from all speculations
> And the awareness that metaphysics is a consequence of being unwell.
> Then I lean back in my chair
> And continue smoking.
> As long as Destiny grants it to me, I will continue smoking.
> (If I married my laundress's daughter
> Perhaps I would be happy.)
> Having seen this, I get up from the chair. I go to the window.
> The man left the tobacconist's (putting change in his trouser pocket?).
> Ah, I know him: it's Esteves without metaphysics.
> (The owner of the tobacconist's arrived at the door.)
> As if by divine instinct, Esteves turned and saw me.
> He waved goodbye to me, I shouted Goodbye, Esteves!, and the universe
> Reconstructed itself for me without ideal or hope, and the owner of the tobacconist's smiled.
